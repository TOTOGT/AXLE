Your First Reel

Gemini for Video · Instagram Edits · Building an Audience, One Post a Day

You don't need to speak perfect English to make a good reel — you need to understand a video well enough to remix it, and post consistently enough for people to notice. This lesson teaches both.

Lesson 05 · Level B1–B2 · 120 minutes · Delivered WPPPW

Your First Reel
Extract, Translate, Remix, Post

Tools: Google Gemini (video understanding) · Instagram Edits (standalone remix app, watermark-free exports)
Remix (v.): To take an existing public video, understand it, and rebuild a short version of it with your own voice, captions, or commentary — not copy it. Every reel in this lesson starts from a video you did not make and ends with something only you could have made.

W — WARM-UP · 10 minutes
What Makes You Stop Scrolling?

In pairs: think of a reel or short video that made you stop scrolling in the last week.

  1. What was it about? Describe it in one sentence.
  2. Was it in your first language, English, or another language? Did the language matter?
  3. How long was it — do you remember? (Most reels that work are under 60 seconds.)

Teacher writes today's goal on the board: By the end of class, everyone will have a script and a plan for their first reel — not necessarily the finished video.

P — PRESENTATION · 25 minutes
The Two Tools: Gemini and Edits

Tool 1 — Gemini understands video. You can paste a public YouTube link into Gemini and ask it to summarize the video, list its key points, or translate what's said into another language. This works because Gemini can watch and listen to the video directly, not just read its title.

Tool 2 — Instagram Edits remixes video. Edits is a free, standalone app for building reels: you can trim clips, add text and captions, and use the "Add Yours" sticker to remix a trend other creators started. Exports have no watermark, and captions can be generated in multiple languages, so the same reel can carry a caption in English and in your first language.

WordMeaning
captionthe text that appears with a post, often explaining or adding to the video
watermarka small logo or mark placed on a video to show which app made it
trenda style, sound, or format many creators are using at the same time
engagementlikes, comments, shares — signs that people are reacting to your post
call to action (CTA)a short instruction telling the viewer what to do next (“follow for more,” “link in bio”)
consistencyposting on a regular schedule, not only when you feel like it
Reality check for students: Gemini's video tools have limits — it generally works with videos up to about 90 minutes long, one video per request, and only with public videos (not private or unlisted). That's more than enough for this lesson, since we're working with short clips.
P — PRACTICE · 35 minutes
Guided Practice — From Video to Script

Follow these steps with the teacher, one at a time. Everyone uses the same example video first, then repeats the steps alone.

#Step
1Find a short public video (60–90 seconds) on a topic you know something about — cooking, sports, a place in your country, anything.
2Copy the video link. Open Gemini and paste it with the prompt: "Summarize this video in 3 bullet points and translate the summary into [your language]."
3Read the summary. Circle 5 words or phrases you didn't know before.
4Ask Gemini a follow-up: "Rewrite this summary as a 20-second script I could say out loud."
5Read the 20-second script aloud to your partner. Time it — is it really 20 seconds?

Comprehension check: Why translate the summary before writing the script, instead of after? (So you understand the content fully in a language you're confident in, before you commit to how you'll say it in English.)

P — PRODUCTION · 35 minutes
Students Produce: Storyboard, Bilingual Caption, 7-Day Plan
Card A — Storyboard (individual)

Sketch or write 3 shots for your reel: Shot 1 (hook — first 3 seconds), Shot 2 (the content), Shot 3 (the CTA). Write one sentence of narration for each shot in English.

Card B — Bilingual Caption (pairs)

Write your caption twice: once in English, once in your first language. They don't need to be word-for-word — they need to work for each audience. Include one hashtag and one CTA in each.

Open Edits app (if available) or plan on paper: Using your storyboard, either build the first draft of your reel in Instagram Edits, or write the exact sequence of actions you'll take when you get home (which clips to trim, where to add text, when to use the "Add Yours" sticker).

7-Day Posting Plan: Write one line for each of the next 7 days — what will you post, or will it be a rest day? Consistency matters more than perfection: a simple reel posted on schedule usually outperforms a perfect reel posted once.

W — WRAP-UP · 15 minutes
Self-Study Quiz + Where This Goes Next

Works in class or alone. Answers below — cover them and check yourself after.

Wrap-Up Quiz — 5 Questions

1. What is the correct order of steps in this lesson's workflow?
  • (a) Post → Summarize → Translate → Script
  • (b) Summarize → Translate → Script → Post
  • (c) Script → Post → Summarize
2. True or False: Gemini can summarize any video, including private ones, if you have the link.
3. What does "watermark-free" mean, and why might it matter for a creator?
4. Write one example of a CTA (call to action) you could use at the end of a reel.
5. True or False: A perfect video posted once will usually build a bigger audience than a simple video posted every day.
Answers: 1—(b) · 2—False, only public videos · 3—No app logo appears on the exported video, so it looks more professional and is easier to repost elsewhere · 4—e.g. "Follow for more" / "Link in bio" · 5—False, consistency usually wins.

Forward link — Lesson 05b: A great reel gets attention. A media kit turns that attention into partnerships — including a Shopee affiliate link you can share. In Lesson 05b, you'll build one.
