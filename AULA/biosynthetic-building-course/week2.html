Week 2 · Ciência dos Materiais: Transformação de Terra / Material Science: Earth Transformation

Binding, compression, and the phase transition from soil to stone.

Learning Objectives

  • Compreender química de aglutinantes naturais (argila, calcário, proteínas)
  • Testar proporções terra-água-aglutinante em laboratório
  • Mapear transição de fase: solo solto → terra comprimida → pedra
  • Conectar à Cap. Ph (Transições de Fase) de Principia Orthogona

Concepts

Binding Agents in Nature

Clay is a natural binder. When mixed with sand and water, clay particles align and create strength through mechanical interlocking — no chemical reaction needed. Lunar regolith (moon dust) behaves the same way. Biological binders go further: termite saliva contains proteins that cross-link soil particles; mycelium (fungal networks) weave through earth; lime (calcium hydroxide from crushed seashells or limestone) chemically bonds. Each creates a different phase: mechanical earth, bio-bonded earth, chemically-bonded earth.

Testing Protocols

How do we know our mixture works? Simple tests: (1) Compression test — squeeze a sample until it breaks, measure force; (2) Water resistance — soak and observe degradation rate; (3) Density — weigh a known volume; (4) Shrinkage — measure change from wet to dry. These tests are accessible to any community, requiring only a scale, water, and time.

Phase Transition Analogy

Just as water freezes at 0°C and becomes ice (a different phase), soil undergoes phase transitions. Wet soil: plastic, flows, no structure. Moist soil (optimal): cohesive, can be compressed, holds shape. Dry soil: rigid, can be load-bearing. Add the right binder and the transformation is permanent. This is physics, not magic. It is reproducible.

Scaling & Variability

No two batches of earth are identical. A builder must test, adjust ratios, document what works. This is why indigenous knowledge is powerful: it encoded generations of local testing. We do the same, but with written records and quantitative tests.

Lab Work (90-min session)

  • Prepare 5 test samples with different clay:sand:binder ratios. Document each: ratio by mass, mixing method, water added, compression date.
  • Compression test (Week 7): stack weight on sample until failure. Record max load. Note failure mode: brittle (shatters) vs. ductile (bends).
  • Shrinkage test: measure sample length/width wet and dry. Calculate % shrinkage. Observe cracks — why? (Differential drying.)
  • Water soak test: submerge samples for 24h. Observe: do they crumble? Soften? Maintain integrity?
  • Document in lab notebook or spreadsheet: all observations, photos, and preliminary conclusions.
✓ Checkpoint · Entrega · Material Matrix: table of all 5 samples with ratios, test results (compression, shrinkage, water), and notes on performance. Which sample performed best? Why? /
