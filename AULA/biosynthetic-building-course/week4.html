Week 4 · Clima e Durabilidade: Construindo para o Local / Climate & Durability: Building for Your Region

Water, temperature, decay — how structures age and how to design for it.

Learning Objectives

  • Analisar clima local e exigências de durabilidade
  • Desenhar proteção contra água (cobertura, drenagem, porosidade)
  • Entender ciclos de umidade e expansão térmica em terra crua
  • Integrar sustentabilidade: materiais locais, manutenção mínima

Concepts

Climate-Adaptive Design

A structure in the tropics (Brazil) faces constant moisture and heat. In temperate zones: freeze-thaw cycles. Desert: UV and wind abrasion. There is no 'one-size-fits-all' building. Indigenous peoples adapted their architecture to climate: Krenac structures are porous (cooling) and sloped (drainage). Moon base designs protect against radiation. We design for your climate: tropical, temperate, or arid.

Water as Enemy & Friend

Water degrades earth structures if not managed. But water is also a key binder: the right moisture content makes earth cohesive. Too wet: it flows. Too dry: it shrinks and cracks. Too wet for too long: it loses strength. The design must shed water (roof overhang, drainage) while allowing the structure to breathe (permeable walls). This is the balance termite mounds achieve: ventilated, yet dry.

Maintenance & Longevity

A biosynthetic earth structure needs minimal maintenance if designed well. Annual inspection of roof (common repair). Occasional mud plaster touch-up (similar to interior paint). No synthetic sealants required — the structure ages gracefully, gradually weathering. This is preferable to concrete, which cracks catastrophically and cannot be repaired without full replacement.

Local Material Economy

Use earth from your site. Use timber from your region. Use straw or other plant fiber if available. This minimizes transport energy and connects the building to its place. It is also cheaper: a participant's structure costs 1/10th that of concrete/brick equivalents because labor (yours, mobilized community) replaces industrial manufacturing.

Lab Work (90-min session)

  • Climate analysis: research your region's rainfall (annual, peak month), temperature range, humidity, and wind speed. Plot on a simple graph.
  • Design roof overhang: how far should the roof extend beyond the wall to protect the earthen surface? (Rule of thumb: at least 60cm in tropics.) Calculate for your latitude and roof pitch.
  • Drainage design: sketch how water will flow away from your structure. Where will it collect? Can you use it (cistern, garden)?
  • Finish options: test 3 external finishes on your Week 2 samples (no finish, earth plaster, lime wash, natural plant oil). Expose to water and observe degradation over 2 weeks.
  • Maintenance schedule: write a simple year-round checklist (inspect roof, repair plaster, etc.).
✓ Checkpoint · Entrega · Climate Report: rainfall/temperature data, roof design with overhang calculation, drainage sketch, finish test results, and a maintenance checklist (one page). How will your structure age over 50 years? /

Reflection Questions

  • How much rain does your region get? How does this inform your design?
  • If your structure is 100 years old, what will have changed?
  • Can your design serve multiple uses over time (shelter → storage → community space)?
