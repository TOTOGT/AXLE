Evidence, Measurement, and Patterns

STAGE 1 — WARM-UP / REVIEW  ·  15 minutes
Activity A: Brainstorm — What Do You Know?

Before class (or now): Teacher writes on board: "CROP CIRCLES"

Ask students (in pairs, 3 min): "What do you know about crop circles? Have you ever heard of them? What are they?"

Students share. Teacher writes responses on the board. Examples: "They appear in fields." "Aliens made them." "Humans make them." "They are beautiful." "They happen at night."

Teacher note: Accept all answers. Don't correct beliefs yet. The goal is to activate prior knowledge and show that students have ideas already.
Activity B: Question for Thinking

Teacher asks: "How can we know if something is TRUE?"

"Is something true because we BELIEVE it? Or because we have EVIDENCE for it?"

Pair discussion (5 min). Then: "Today, we will learn to see the difference between BELIEF and EVIDENCE. We will watch a video. We will measure shapes. And we will think like scientists."

Activity C: Video Introduction (Preview, 3 min)

Show title card: "What really creates Crop Circles?"

Ask: "Do you think this video will say: (A) Aliens made them, OR (B) Humans made them, OR (C) We're not sure yet?"

Quick vote by show of hands. Keep track on the board. Tell them: "Let's watch and see if you were right."

STAGE 2 — INTRODUCTION  ·  15 minutes
Activity: Vocabulary Preview + Sentence Frames

Introduce 10 KEY VOCABULARY WORDS. Students copy into notebooks or handout:

WordMeaningExample Sentence (B-level)
Circlea round shapeA crop circle is a _____ in a field.
Diameterthe line across a circle through the centerThe _____ of this circle is 100 meters.
Ratiothe relationship between two numbers (e.g., 2:1)The _____ of circle A to circle B is 1:2.
Symmetry / Symmetricwhen both sides are the same; balancedThis shape has _____ because both sides are the same.
Patternshapes or things that repeatDo you see a _____ in this crop circle?
Measurementthe size or distance of somethingWe use a ruler to take a _____ of the circle.
Evidenceinformation that proves something is trueWe have _____ that humans can make crop circles.
Hoaxa trick or false claimIn 1991, two men confessed to a _____ . They had made crop circles by hand.
Precise / Preciselyvery exact and accurateThe _____ of this measurement is important.
Phenomenonan unusual event or observationCrop circles are a _____ that people want to understand.

SENTENCE FRAMES FOR B-LEVEL SPEAKERS (post on wall or provide as handout):

• "I think _____ because _____."
• "The evidence shows that _____."
• "This circle has _____ (diameter / symmetry / pattern)."
• "I'm not sure if _____ is true."
• "We don't have evidence yet, but _____."
STAGE 3 — PRESENTATION  ·  25 minutes
Activity A: Documentary Viewing (Segment 1 + 2)

SEGMENT 1: Watch 00:00–09:00 (The Mystery) — 9 minutes

Students have a simple note-taking sheet:

What did I see?
• Where are the crop circles? _______________
• What shapes do they have? _______________
• Are they big or small? _______________

SEGMENT 2: Watch 23:31–28:00 (The Hoax) — 4.5 minutes

Students watch how humans make crop circles. KEY POINTS TO EMPHASIZE:

  • Two men named Doug and Dave created crop circles
  • They used simple tools: wooden planks and string
  • They could make a 50-meter circle in daylight
  • This PROVES that humans CAN make crop circles
Teacher tip: Pause the video after the hoax demonstration. Ask: "Can humans make crop circles?" Wait for answers. Make it clear: YES, humans can. Evidence: Doug & Dave demonstration.
Activity B: Comprehension Check

Ask 5 simple yes/no questions:

  • Can humans make crop circles? (YES / NO)
  • Did Doug and Dave confess? (YES / NO)
  • Did they use alien technology? (YES / NO)
  • Can they do it with simple tools? (YES / NO)
  • Is this evidence that ALL crop circles are human-made? (YES / NO)

Discuss answer to question 5. (Answer: NO — this proves humans CAN make them, but doesn't prove humans made every single one.)

STAGE 4 — GUIDED PRACTICE  ·  25 minutes
Activity A: The Four Glyphs — Measurement Practice

Show the four crop circle photographs (below). Students will measure them.

Glyph 1: Grovely Wood, June 15, 2026 — 3 circles in triangle
Glyph 2: Kingweston, June 21, 2026 — 8 circles in pattern
Glyph 3: Zürcher Weinland, June 23 — circles arranged in pattern
Glyph 4: Etchilhampton Hill, June 24/25 — 5 circles in circle

MEASUREMENT TASK (in pairs, 12 min):

Give each pair:

  • A printed photograph of one glyph
  • A ruler (with cm markings)
  • A measurement worksheet (below)
MEASUREMENT WORKSHEET — B-Level

Your glyph is: ________________________

1. Count the circles:
How many circles are in this glyph? _____ circles

2. Measure the outer circle:
Using a ruler, measure the diameter (the line across the circle).
Diameter = _____ cm

3. Measure an inner circle:
Diameter = _____ cm

4. Calculate the ratio:
Outer diameter ÷ Inner diameter = _____

5. Write a sentence (use a frame):
"This glyph has _____ circles. The diameter is _____ cm. The ratio is _____."

While students measure: Teacher circulates. Ask questions: "What do you see?" "How many circles?" "Is the measurement exact or approximate?" "Why is measurement important?"

Differentiation: For lower B-level: provide pre-drawn circles on the photograph with measurement lines. For upper B-level: have them estimate margin of error (±5–10%) and discuss why measurements from photos might not be perfectly accurate.
Activity B: Vocabulary Application — Sentence Building (8 min)

Complete these sentences with vocabulary from today:

  • A crop circle is a _____ (circle / pattern / measurement) in a field.
  • The _____ (diameter / ratio / symmetry) is the line across the circle.
  • A _____ (hoax / phenomenon / measurement) is a trick.
  • We use _____ (evidence / symmetry / precision) to prove something is true.
  • This glyph has _____ (symmetry / ratio / diameter) because both sides are the same.

Teacher reviews answers whole-class (5 min). Write correct answers on board.

STAGE 5 — COMMUNICATIVE PRACTICE  ·  20 minutes
Activity: Discussion Cards — Group Conversations

Divide class into 4 groups. Each group gets one discussion card. Students read the card, discuss, then report to the class.

Card A — Humans or Aliens?

Fact: We have EVIDENCE that humans can make crop circles. Doug and Dave made them with wooden planks and string.

Question: Does this mean ALL crop circles are made by humans? Why or why not? Use this frame: "I think _____ because we have _____ that _____."

Card B — Belief vs. Evidence

Fact: Some people BELIEVE aliens made crop circles. Other people have EVIDENCE that humans made them.

Question: What is the difference between a belief and evidence? Can both be true? Use this frame: "A belief is _____, but evidence is _____."

Card C — The 90/10 Question

Fact: About 90% of crop circles are proven to be human-made. About 10% remain unexplained.

Question: If we know how humans make crop circles, why do some people still believe in aliens? Use this frame: "People believe in _____ because _____."

Card D — Pattern & Symmetry

Fact: The four crop circles from June 2026 all have geometric symmetry (circles arranged in balanced patterns).

Question: Why might someone create a symmetrical pattern? Is symmetry beautiful? Why do humans like symmetry? Use this frame: "I think symmetry is important because _____."

Group Report (3 min per group, 12 min total): Each group reports their answer to the class. Other students listen and ask one follow-up question.

Teacher role: Ask follow-up questions if groups are stuck. Model complete sentences. Write key vocabulary on the board as they use it.
STAGE 6 — EVALUATION  ·  15 minutes
Activity: Exit Writing — Reflection or Explanation (Choose One)

Students choose ONE of these tasks and write 4–6 sentences. (Provide sentence frames below.)

OPTION A — Describe What You Learned:
What did you learn today about crop circles? What is the evidence? What do we not know yet?

Frame: "Today I learned that _____. We have evidence that _____. We don't know yet if _____. I think this is important because _____."

OPTION B — Your Opinion:
Do you think humans or aliens (or unknown forces) made the 2026 crop circles? Why?

Frame: "I think the crop circles were made by _____ because _____. The evidence shows _____. I'm not sure about _____ because we need more _____."

OPTION C — Measurement:
Explain how to measure a crop circle. What tools do you need? What do you measure? Why is it important?

Frame: "To measure a crop circle, you need _____. First, you _____. Then, you _____. This is important because _____."
Assessment note: Accept simple, grammatically imperfect sentences. Focus on vocabulary use and clarity of thought. A B-level student who uses "diameter," "ratio," and "evidence" correctly = success.
STAGE 7 — APPLICATION  ·  5 minutes
Activity: Reflection & Connection to Life

Ask students (whole group or pairs):

  • In your life, when do you need to measure something? (examples: cooking, building, sewing, work)
  • How do you know if something is TRUE? What kind of evidence do you need?
  • Did anything in today's lesson surprise you?

If time permits: Ask 2–3 students to share. Celebrate language use.

"Today, you measured like scientists. You listened to evidence. You thought like mathematicians. That's English AND thinking at the same time."

Continue the story: Part 2 — Mandelbrot's Crop Circle → — a real mathematician, a magazine letter, and a field in England that made headlines in 1991.

Differentiation Strategies

For Lower B-Level (B1)

• Provide images with labels and measurements already marked
• Accept shorter written responses (2–3 sentences)
• Use only 5–6 vocabulary words instead of 10
• Pair with stronger English speaker for discussion
• Simplify exit writing to: "Humans CAN / CANNOT make crop circles because _____"
For Upper B-Level (B2)

• Have students calculate multiple ratios and compare
• Ask about measurement error (±5–10%)
• Extend writing to full paragraphs
• Have them create their OWN discussion question
• Introduce "anomaly" and "hypothesis" (advanced vocabulary)
