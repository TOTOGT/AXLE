The WorldQuant Top 10

Country Strategies in the IQC — Quality vs. Quantity

Lesson 04 asked what the leaderboard says. This lesson asks what the leaderboard means. Using the same data, students calculate a new number — score per participant — and discover that the USA’s problem in 2026 is not only that it has fewer people in the competition.

Lesson 06 · Level B1–B2 · 90 minutes · Delivered WPPPW

Country Strategies in the IQC
Advanced Reading of Competition Data — A Companion to Lesson 04

Source: WorldQuant BRAIN Platform — Live Leaderboard (May 2026 snapshot, same dataset as Lesson 04)
Efficiency (n.): In this lesson, a country’s aggregate score divided by its number of participants — how much each researcher contributes, on average. A country can rank high by having many participants (scale) or by having each participant score high (efficiency). Most winning strategies mix both.

W — WARM-UP · 10 minutes
Two Numbers, One Question

Teacher writes on the board: China: 24,454 participants, 53,568,436 points. USA: 2,331 participants, 674,966 points.

  1. China has about 10× the participants of the USA. Roughly how many times more points does China have?
  2. Is China’s advantage only about having more people? How could you check?
  3. If you ran the USA team, would you rather have 10× more participants, or participants who each score 10× higher? Why?
P — PRESENTATION · 20 minutes
Introducing Score-per-Participant + Comprehension Check

Teacher introduces the formula: score ÷ participants = score per participant. Model it together with China: 53,568,436 ÷ 24,454 ≈ 2,191 points per participant. Then the USA: 674,966 ÷ 2,331 ≈ 290 points per participant.

WordMeaning
ratioa relationship between two numbers showing how many times one contains the other
efficiencyhow much output is produced per unit of input — here, points per participant
scalethe size of an operation — here, the number of participants
outliera data point far above or below the rest of the pattern
hedge (v.)to soften a claim so it isn’t stated as 100% certain (this may suggest…)

Comprehension check (pairs, 5 min): Is China’s per-participant score (2,191) higher or lower than the USA’s (290)? By roughly how many times? (Answer: about 7–8 times higher — so the gap is not only about scale.)

P — PRODUCTION · 25 minutes
Students Produce: Discussion → Written Argument
Card A — China's Outlier Number

China’s 2,191 points per participant is far above everyone else. What could explain a number this high — individual skill, team submissions counted per member, more experienced researchers, something else? You don’t have to be sure. Practice hedging: “This may suggest…”, “One possible explanation is…”

Card B — Two Strategies

Nigeria has 11,701 participants but only 83 points each. Taiwan has 1,747 participants but 994 points each. Both are “behind” China. Which country is in a better position to climb the leaderboard by 2027, and why?

Written Production (choose one, 150–200 words):

  1. Strategy Memo: Advise the USA team. Should it recruit more participants (scale), train existing participants to submit better alphas (efficiency), or both? Use at least two numbers from the table and at least one hedge.
  2. Country Case Study: Pick any country except the USA and China. Explain its position using both total score and score-per-participant. What does each number tell you that the other doesn’t?
W — WRAP-UP · 10 minutes
Self-Study Quiz + Forward Link

Works in class or alone. Answers below — cover them and check yourself after.

Wrap-Up Quiz — 5 Questions

1. What is score-per-participant a measure of?
  • (a) total scale
  • (b) efficiency
  • (c) rank
2. True or False: The USA's main problem in 2026 is only that it has too few participants.
3. Which country has the highest score-per-participant in the Top 10?
4. Fill in the hedge: “____________ China's institutions submit team alphas that count for each member.” (this may suggest / it is possible that)
5. Nigeria has more participants than Vietnam but a lower score-per-participant. Which country is winning on scale, and which is winning on efficiency?
Answers: 1—(b) efficiency · 2—False, its per-participant score also lags most of the Top 10 · 3—China (2,191) · 4—either hedge works grammatically · 5—Nigeria wins on scale, Vietnam wins on efficiency.

Teacher Notes — This lesson only works if students actually do the division in Practice — don't hand them the answer key early. Calculators or phones are fine; the skill being taught is reading a ratio and reasoning about what it hides, not mental math.

China's outlier number: The lesson deliberately does not resolve why China's per-participant score is so high. Real leaderboards contain numbers whose causes aren't fully known from the data alone — that's a genuine research/data-literacy lesson, not a gap in the material.

Self-study use: This lesson was designed to also work without a teacher present. A student can complete Warm-Up through Production alone using a calculator and the answer key in Practice, then self-grade with the Wrap-Up quiz.
