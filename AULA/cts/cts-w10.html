Developing an AV Project Scope

The paperwork that turns a survey into an approved, fundable project
CTS Prep · Week 10 of 16
Course: CTS — Audiovisual & IT Systems  ·  AVIXA Ch. 15

Once a site is evaluated and stakeholder needs are understood, that information has to become a document other people can approve, budget, and hold the project accountable to. A scope of work (SOW) defines exactly what will and won't be delivered — which rooms, which systems, which capabilities — in specific enough language that there's no ambiguity later about whether something was included.

A bill of materials (BOM) lists every piece of equipment the design requires, with model numbers and quantities, tying the scope to actual cost. A project timeline or milestone schedule breaks the work into phases (design approval, procurement, installation, commissioning, training/handoff) with target dates, which matters enormously in an environment like a courthouse where downtime has to be scheduled around active court proceedings, not just convenience.

A change order is the formal mechanism for handling scope changes after a project has already been approved — if a stakeholder decides mid-project that they now also want overflow-room streaming, that's a change order, not a silent scope expansion (sometimes called scope creep), which is one of the most common ways AV projects go over budget and past deadline without anyone officially deciding to allow it.

Even as a technician rather than a project manager, understanding this document trail matters: it's the reference for what “correct” looks like when troubleshooting or verifying an installation, and it's the paper trail that protects both the Court and the contractor/technician if a dispute arises about what was promised.

Key Terms

Scope of work (SOW) — document defining exactly what a project will deliver
Bill of materials (BOM) — itemized equipment list with quantities and models
Milestone schedule — project timeline broken into phased target dates
Change order — formal process for approved scope changes mid-project
Scope creep — unapproved, informal expansion of project requirements
Practice Questions
  1. A stakeholder asks mid-project for an additional feature not in the original scope of work. What is the correct process, and what's the risk of just adding it informally?
    Show answerA formal change order documenting the new requirement, cost, and schedule impact; adding it informally (scope creep) risks budget overruns and timeline slips with no accountability trail.
  2. Why does a courthouse AV project's milestone schedule need to account for active court proceedings, unlike a typical office renovation?
    Show answerInstallation and downtime windows must be scheduled around when courtrooms are actually in active use, since disrupting a live proceeding isn't an option the way it might be in an ordinary office.
  3. What is the practical difference between a scope of work and a bill of materials?
    Show answerThe SOW defines what capabilities/deliverables the project includes; the BOM is the itemized equipment list (with models and quantities) needed to actually build those deliverables.
