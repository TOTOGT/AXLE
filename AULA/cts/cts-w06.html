Electrical Systems & Wireless Radio Systems

Power safety fundamentals and the RF spectrum AV borrows from
CTS Prep · Week 06 of 16
Course: CTS — Audiovisual & IT Systems  ·  AVIXA Ch. 9–10

Electrical systems. AV racks draw meaningful power and are sensitive to electrical problems that wouldn't bother a lamp. Dedicated circuits (a circuit not shared with other building loads) prevent AV gear from browning out when, say, an HVAC unit kicks on. Proper grounding prevents both safety hazards and a specific AV problem — ground loops, which show up as an audible hum in audio systems caused by a small voltage difference between two ground points. A UPS (uninterruptible power supply) protects against brief outages and gives equipment time for a clean shutdown, while power conditioners smooth out voltage fluctuations that would otherwise stress sensitive electronics.

This is also where the job posting's “manual labor” and “moving and lifting weights” requirements connect — technicians frequently work inside equipment racks and near live electrical panels, and basic electrical safety awareness (lockout/tagout concepts, not touching energized equipment, proper cable routing away from heat) is a baseline expectation even without being a licensed electrician.

Wireless radio systems. Wireless microphones operate in specific radio frequency bands (commonly VHF or UHF), and multiple wireless mics in the same room must be frequency-coordinated so they don't interfere with each other or with other RF sources in the building. Lectrosonics, named directly in the Newark posting, is a leading manufacturer of professional wireless microphone and IR (infrared) assistive-listening systems — the latter being especially relevant in a courtroom setting, where accessibility for hearing-impaired jurors, witnesses, or spectators is often a legal requirement, not just a convenience.

IR-based assistive listening is distinct from IR remote control (Week 5) — it uses infrared light to transmit an audio signal to a receiver worn by a listener, rather than transmitting short control commands to a device.

Job posting tie-in: “Lectrosonics” is named directly in the posting's desirable-knowledge list, and “wireless and infrared audio” appears in the required systems knowledge.

Key Terms

Dedicated circuit — electrical circuit not shared with unrelated loads
Grounding — electrical safety connection; prevents ground-loop hum
UPS — uninterruptible power supply
Power conditioner — smooths voltage fluctuations
Frequency coordination — preventing wireless mic RF interference
Assistive listening (IR) — infrared audio delivery for accessibility
Practice Questions
  1. A courtroom sound system develops a persistent low hum whenever two racks in different rooms are connected by a shared audio cable. What is this a classic symptom of, and what fixes it?
    Show answerA ground loop — caused by a voltage difference between two separate ground points. Fixes include a ground-isolation transformer/DI box on the connection, or ensuring proper single-point grounding design.
  2. Why would a courtroom deploy multiple wireless microphones need careful frequency coordination rather than each just being set to any open channel?
    Show answerUncoordinated wireless mics can interfere with each other (intermodulation, dropouts) or with other RF devices in the building; coordination software assigns compatible frequencies to avoid conflicts.
  3. Why is IR-based assistive listening particularly relevant to a U.S. District Court AV/IT posting specifically, beyond general AV good practice?
    Show answerCourts have legal accessibility obligations (e.g. under the ADA) to accommodate hearing-impaired jurors, witnesses, and the public — assistive listening systems are often a compliance requirement, not just a nice-to-have.
