The One Instrument Ever Pointed at Nepal
Five jurisdictions admit people displaced by disaster. None of them is general — and the one that ever covered Nepal was designated in 2015, held by some twelve thousand people, and then withdrawn

Last week this paper argued that a fold leaves no room to respond, and that the interval before it is the only part anyone controls. The obvious follow-up arrived immediately: and if a valley cannot be made safe in the interval, where do its people go? That question has a legal answer rather than a moral one, and this desk went to find it.

There are five instruments worth naming. The United States operates Temporary Protected Status under INA §244, which covers environmental disaster explicitly. Brazil names “major calamity” and “environmental disaster” in the statute itself, at Lei 13.445/2017, art. 14 §3º, consolidated since 1 January 2026 under a single ministerial instrument. Argentina runs a standing humanitarian visa for people displaced by socio-natural disasters, created by Disposición DNM 891/2022. Australia and Tuvalu have the world’s only bilateral climate-mobility treaty, the Falepili Union, in force since August 2024. The European Union has its Temporary Protection Directive, activated exactly once, for Ukraine.

None of them is general

Every one is either designated country by country, limited by region, or bilateral. Argentina’s covers twenty-three countries in Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean. The Falepili pathway admits 280 Tuvaluans a year. Brazil’s article is open but requires an act of designation that has not been made. A Nepali citizen displaced on 26 August 2026 had nothing, anywhere, to invoke as of right.

That would be the whole story if Nepal were an untested case. It is not. After the 2015 earthquake the United States designated Nepal for TPS on 24 June 2015; approximately 12,700 Nepali nationals held that status and about 5,500 had already become lawful permanent residents. The designation was later terminated.

Two dates for that termination are in circulation — a Federal Register notice of 6 June 2025 setting an effective date of 5 August 2025, and a report of a Departmental announcement on 15 August 2026. This paper carries both, dated and attributed, and has not chosen between them; reconciling them is an open item, and litigation is the obvious candidate. What is not in doubt is that the instrument was closed before the Lhende flooded.

A sentiment cannot be refused by name. An article number can — which is the only reason to write one down.

So the finding is not that the world lacks machinery. It is that the machinery works, has been pointed at this country once, and was withdrawn. That is a fact about willingness, not about capability, and it is a sharper thing to report than an absence would have been. An instrument never tried can be proposed. One tried and taken back has to be argued for against whatever took it back.

The nearest thing to a general instrument

Argentina’s programme is the one worth studying. It was built before the emergency rather than during one, it is permanent rather than exceptional, it is backed by civil-society sponsorship for the first year, and it converts to permanent residence after three. Its only limit is a list of countries — and in the whole apparatus of admission, a list is the cheapest thing there is to change.

On the question of destination, since this paper is edited by a Brazilian: not the coast and not the south. The Planalto Central. Goiás sits at around a thousand metres on the cerrado plateau, outside the cyclone tracks, effectively aseismic, and its floods are local rather than the kind that displaced Rio Grande do Sul in 2024. It has a hazard of its own, and a page that names a destination has to name that too: the dry season, drought and fire, from August into September. The state’s two letters, as it happens, are GO.

None of this is legal advice, and none of it argues that anyone should leave Nepal. The argument is narrower and, this desk thinks, more useful. “They should be able to go somewhere” is a sentiment, and a sentiment cannot be filed, checked, or refused. “Lei 13.445/2017, art. 14 §3º, designation by joint ministerial act” can be all three. As these events become more frequent, the difference between the two sentences is most of what preparation means.

The pattern under all seven

Four of these were produced by a search that failed and was reported as an absence. A case-sensitive file search missed a file whose name differed by one capital letter. A path-trimming routine that removes characters rather than a prefix turned sixty-nine working links into phantom broken ones. A site-absolute path was read as a relative one, and a front page in perfect health was reported as thirty-four links dead of thirty-five.

None of those was a wrong answer about the corpus. Each was a correct answer about a search, presented as a fact about the world. The rule now standing in this desk’s working file reads: a failed search is evidence about the search. Write what is known — I did not find X by this method — and look again by a method of a different shape before it reaches a page.

A false absence is more expensive than a missing finding. The finding can still be made later; the deleted work cannot; and in between it sits on the page under the author’s name, telling readers his own work does not exist.

That last clause is not hypothetical. One page of Volume I currently informs its readers that two results are unverified sketches. The repository holding them contains three hundred and thirteen declarations, and the file in question opens by describing its own principal result as complete and free of sorry. The correction is the author’s to make and is listed here as open.

A Filter Applied One Quantity at a Time
An earlier paper sorted this desk’s constants into those a spectrum measurement could refute and those it could not. The sorting was correct and the procedure was blind

The test was stated plainly: a quantity is exposed to a spectrum measurement if and only if it survives multiplying every energy in the problem by an arbitrary positive constant. Applied to eight quantities, it left two.

Applied one quantity at a time, which is how it was applied, it cannot see a combination. The limit-cycle period $T^*=2\pi$ is a scale. The transverse Lyapunov exponent $\mu_{\max}=-2$ is a scale. Their product is not: rescale time by $a$ and one is divided by $a$ while the other is multiplied by it, leaving

$\mu_{\max}\,T^* = -4\pi$

fixed — the Floquet exponent over one period, dimensionless, and precisely the sort of thing a spectrum resolves. Two of eight becomes three of nine, and the missing one is the standard observable of any limit cycle.

The correction also separated two objects the paper had been calling by one name. The n-bonacci roots — $\varphi, \eta, \Delta \to \tau = 2$ — are dominant roots of a characteristic polynomial: algebraic constants, exposed to no measurement. The gap spectrum — separations between eigenvalues, Floquet multipliers, ratios of rates — is dimensionless by construction. The word “spectral” had been carrying both, and the second inherited the first’s verdict.

Which relocates the open work. Circadian rhythm, cardiac dynamics, autophagy, neural oscillation: limit-cycle systems whose measurable invariant is the multiplier, and where rising complexity registers as the gap changing. Those chapters were written before the filter existed and have not been read against it. The register should be expected to grow.

Twelve, and only twelve

A second paper this week asks what happens on twelve sites under six-fold symmetry, and finds that the only non-constant surviving harmonic is the folding frequency itself — the frequency at which a sampled spectrum reflects. The hexagon, on that reading, does not appear near the fold; in the sample domain it is the fold.

The first draft called that structural. It is not. For six-fold symmetry on $N$ sites the surviving modes are the multiples of six below $N/2$, and that set reduces to the folding frequency alone exactly when $N/2 = 6$:

So it is a characterisation of twelve. The framework’s twelve-slot phase vector has looked like an arbitrary resolution; on this reading it is the unique site count at which six-fold symmetry carries no harmonic content except the fold. A stronger claim than the one originally made, and a narrower one.

The same paper had answered its own strongest objection too confidently. The fold of catastrophe theory is a smooth map with a vanishing derivative; the fold of sampling theory is a reflection on a finite set, where there is no derivative to vanish. They share a quotient — a space divided by an involution with isolated fixed points — and not a normal form. The paper now claims the quotient.

A correspondence is cheap until it predicts something that could have come out otherwise. This one does: twelve sections per revolution resolve transverse frequencies to six per revolution, so a torus bifurcation faster than that folds a real frequency onto a false one and the discrete model fails outright.

Versailles, the night she left
No. 3 The Unfinished Cage
Pierre et Mademoiselle, by Pablo Nogueira Grossi  ·  See the note on fact & fiction, below

She had expected the dark. What she had not expected was the noise.

Even at this hour — past midnight, the court long since retired to whatever version of sleep a palace of that size permitted — the construction never fully stopped. Somewhere off toward the new wing, torches moved in a slow constellation as night crews shifted stone that the king's masons had decided could not wait for daylight. The whole western approach was a wound of scaffolding and mud, half-raised walls standing pale and skeletal against the dark, waiting for a roof that would not exist for another decade.

It was, she thought, walking fast with her case held close against her borrowed cloak, the strangest possible place to be afraid of being seen. A palace under this much construction had a thousand shadows a fleeing woman could disappear into, and a thousand workmen too exhausted, too indifferent, too used to strange lights and strange movements in the dark to look twice at one more figure hurrying somewhere it wasn't their business to ask about.

⁂

She had heard the story, the way everyone at court eventually did, in the particular hushed, half-mocking, half-reverent tone reserved for the things that explained a king without anyone having to say so directly: how he had been ten years old, asleep in a Paris palace, when the mob came. How they had climbed into his very bedroom to make certain he had not fled the city in the night, and how he had lain there, a child, forced to feign sleep while armed strangers loomed over him deciding, in effect, whether he still belonged to them.

He had never gone back to that city the same way again. And now, seventeen years later, he was building this — walls thick enough that no mob would ever again cross them uninvited, a court so total and so gilded that every noble in France would eventually be made to live inside it, watched, accounted for, never quite out of his sight.

Marie understood, picking her way past a stack of unfinished cornices taller than she was, that she was walking through the exact shape of his fear, made permanent in stone. He had built an entire palace so that nothing like her — nothing that could simply vanish into a night he hadn't authorized — would ever be possible again.

She was, perhaps, the last thing to slip out of Versailles before the cage was finished closing.

⁂

A foreman's lantern swung her way once, near the edge of the unfinished orangery, and she froze instinctively, every calculation Fermat had ever taught her running at once through her mind — stand still, don't run, running is what gets noticed — but the man barely glanced at her, muttered something about the night shift's water ration, and moved on. To him she was simply one more indistinct shape among the hundreds that moved through this endless, half-built site at every hour God made. The king's obsession with control had, in its earliest and clumsiest years, produced exactly the chaos a person could disappear into.

By the time she reached the tree line at the edge of the grounds, the torches behind her had shrunk to a loose, scattered scatter of light, workers still raising walls for a king who would spend the rest of his reign trying to make sure no one ever left this place again without his knowing.

She did not look back a second time. She had already seen enough: an unfinished palace, half cage and half ruin, and a king asleep somewhere inside it who did not yet know that the one thing his stone and his guards and his obsessive, wounded need for control could not do was hold onto a woman who had already decided, calmly and completely, to be gone.

Fact & Fiction · the ledger for No. 3

- 1632 — the Society of Jesus, through its Revisors General under Jacob Bidermann, forbade the teaching of indivisibles in its colleges. Fermat was 31. - 1632 — Galileo summoned to Rome. Abjured 1633; house arrest until his death in 1642. - 1651 — indivisibles placed on the Society's list of permanently forbidden doctrines; four of the listed propositions bear directly on the infinitely small. Jesuit tracts against them by Guldin, Bettini and Tacquet, who wrote that the method made such war on geometry that it must itself be destroyed. Effects still felt in Jesuit mathematics a century later.

Invented. Her flight, the borrowed cloak, the hour. What is documented is that Versailles was a building site through the 1660s, with work continuing on the western approach and the new wing long after the court was in residence.
