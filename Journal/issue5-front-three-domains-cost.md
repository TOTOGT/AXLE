## WHERE THE MONEY IS

### Three domains, one operator, and an honest accounting of what it would be worth

---

In June the desk deposited a paper that maps three unrelated biological
systems onto a single operator chain: aminoglycoside-resistance
riboswitches, bridge amplification on Illumina flow cells, and
microtubule curvature under volatile anaesthesia.
[doi:10.5281/zenodo.20560849](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20560849)

The mathematics is machine-checked. The Coherence Bridge Theorem
(Thm 5.4) is verified in Lean 4 / Mathlib4 with zero open `sorry`
obligations in the core chain, and it says something specific: any two
dm³ systems sharing the invariants (μ_max, ω, κ\*) are categorically
equivalent under explicit contact morphisms. A riboswitch and a flow
cell are the same object wearing different clothes.

That is the proved part. This page is about the unproved part — because
readers keep asking what it would be worth, and the desk would rather
answer the question with its own arithmetic than let someone else
invent a number.

---

### The three burdens, as documented

**Antimicrobial resistance.** The CDC, with the University of Utah
School of Medicine, put the US healthcare cost of six of the most urgent
resistant threats at **more than $4.6 billion a year**, against roughly
2.8 million resistant infections annually. Domain 1 of the paper is an
aminoglycoside-resistance riboswitch.

**Sequencing.** The US next-generation sequencing market ran somewhere
between **$2.85 and $3.66 billion in 2025**, depending on whose
methodology you accept. Domain 2 is bridge amplification — the surface
process that determines how much of a flow cell yields usable clusters.

**Postoperative neurocognitive injury.** A national extrapolation from
Medicare data on major elective surgery attributes **$32.9 billion a
year** to postoperative delirium, at $44,291 per affected patient per
year, most of it inside the first ninety days. The wider literature
ranges from $26–42 billion for the postoperative case up to $150 billion
for delirium generally. Domain 3 is microtubule curvature dynamics under
volatile anaesthesia.

---

### The finding the desk did not expect

Set those three side by side and the answer is lopsided.

**Roughly nine-tenths of the addressable burden sits in Domain 3** — the
anaesthesia domain — and almost none of it in Domain 2, which is the
domain with the sharpest prediction. The NGS figure is a *market*, not a
waste: it is revenue for work that gets done, and a yield improvement
claims a fraction of a fraction of it. Sequencing is where the framework
is easiest to falsify. Anaesthesia is where it would matter.

Those are not the same domain, and the desk had assumed they were.

---

### What would have to be true

The paper states falsifiable predictions with explicit protocols. It
does not demonstrate a saving, and **two domain axioms remain openly
unproved**, which the paper says on its own front matter rather than in
a footnote.

So the honest form of the claim is conditional, and the conditional is
steep:

> A one-percent reduction in the documented postoperative delirium
> burden is about **$330 million a year**. To speak in billions you need
> a double-digit percentage effect, sustained, in a population where
> nothing else has produced one.

Nobody has shown that. The desk has not shown it. What the desk has
shown is that the curvature threshold κ\* is the same object in all
three systems, and that if κ\* governs microtubule behaviour under
volatile agents the way it governs cluster formation on a flow cell,
then the mechanism is *addressable* — which is a different and much
smaller word than *addressed*.

---

### The split that matters for the bench

There is a second asymmetry in the table, and it is the one the desk is
acting on.

**The money is in Domain 3. The testable work is in Domains 1 and 2 —
and both of those are nucleic acid.** Domain 1 is an RNA conformational
switch. Domain 2 is DNA on a glass surface. Neither requires an
operating theatre, an anaesthesia protocol, or a cohort followed for
ninety days. Both can be run in a molecular biology laboratory that
already exists, on equipment that is already there, by people who are
already doing this work for other reasons.

That is the whole reason the Coherence Bridge Theorem is worth having.
It is what lets a flow-cell result carry into a domain nobody would fund
you to enter directly. Prove κ\* in the cheap system; the morphism does
the rest, or it fails and you have learned that too.

**The desk is looking for a nucleic-acid bench to test P1–P3 in, and is
offering to do the mathematics for it.** The Lean sources are open, the
protocols are in the paper with their falsification criteria attached,
and the correspondence address is at the foot of this page. In
Portuguese, if that is easier.

---

### Why publish the ceiling and the floor together

Because the alternative is what the field usually does: quote the
$32.9 billion, omit the conditional, and let the reader supply the
optimism.

The desk's position is that a framework which will not state its own
falsification criteria is not worth the arithmetic. Domain 2 is the one
to test first — it is cheap, it is fast, and prediction P1 says κ\* is
derivable from DNA persistence length and primer spacing **with no free
parameters**. A single well-designed flow-cell experiment kills it or
keeps it.

If it survives there, the Coherence Bridge is what carries it to the
domain where the money is.

That is the whole argument, and it fits on one page.
