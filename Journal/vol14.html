Eight Ninths, and One Equivalence Withdrawn
A new proof puts more than 8/9 of the zeta zeros on the critical line or simple. In the same week Book 4 took back its own claim of an equivalence with the Riemann Hypothesis.

On 16 October Cristian Cobeli and Alexandru Zaharescu posted a proof, with no unproved hypothesis, that the lower limiting proportion of nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function that are simple or lie on the critical line is greater than 8/9, zeros counted with multiplicity. The constant their method produces is about 0.88893. The previous unconditional bound in the paper’s own account is Lamzouri’s 0.88762. This is a statement about a proportion of the zeros. It says nothing about the remaining ninth, and it is not the Riemann Hypothesis. Page 3 sets it beside two other outside results.

Book 4 went the other way. Chapters 13, 14 and 14A had described a contact condition as equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis. That equivalence is not shown, and on 19 October the claim was withdrawn: the RH paper gained a new §4.9, the chapters carry dated correction boxes, and a six-check script reproduces the numbers in about 47 seconds. Page 4 lists each change.

A proof checked by people, an answer checked by a script, a theorem checked by a kernel: three different things that all get called verified.

That is the thread of this issue. Page 2 puts the series’ own count of kernel-checked theorems next to what the count does and does not establish. Page 5 is the open desk: what is still unchecked.

None of the Lean was rebuilt for this issue. The Reading Room, Page 6, carries Part 9 of the serial, set from its source.

The one number to carry

8/9 is 0.88889. The new constant is about 0.88893, so the margin is roughly 0.00004. The gain over the earlier bound is about 0.0013. The distance from 8/9 to certainty is the remaining 0.111.

Three Levels of “Verified”
What the series’ own Lean tally establishes, and what it leaves open

The count of kernel-checked theorems is the number people reach for first. It answers one question only: did the Lean kernel accept the file. Two further questions sit behind it. Does the theorem say what its name and docstring say, and does the model it describes match a real system?

Level 1: the kernel accepts it. Of the 82 Volume I theorems, 29 are named in geometry’s foundational documents and 53 are not. No mapping from a numbered Result to a Lean name exists in either repository. So “Volume I is machine-verified” is today a statement about a file in another repository.

Level 2: the content is checked. A vacuity scan flags conclusions that are trivially inhabited, such as True or ∃ _, True. It ships with five deliberate vacuous specimens and one honest control, so the instrument is tested before its verdict counts. It returned 0 flagged over the 82. It has not been run on geometry’s 31 or on io, which are kernel-checked and unverified for content.

Level 3: the model meets data. The dm³ toy model of Volume II has a contraction bound τ = √(c/κnoise) (Vol II Thm 3.2), with V = ρ² canonical. Volume I’s (c, κ) = (1, ¼) is stated without derivation, and no erratum is written until its origin is found. A row in the translation table moves from analogy to model to established only on that field’s data or a derivation, and nobody yet knows how close the toy model is to the empirical systems.

Three Papers, Three Kinds of Checking
Read for what each one certifies. First pages and abstracts only except where stated.
PaperWhat it claimsWhat checks it
Cobeli & Zaharescu, arXiv:2610.23467 (16 Oct 2026)More than 8/9 of the nontrivial zeta zeros are simple or on the critical line, unconditionally, counted with multiplicity.A proof read by referees and readers. The paper credits an earlier unconditional bound to a proof written by Claude and verified by Alpoge and Furman.
Glazer et al., FrontierMath, arXiv:2411.04872 (v7, Dec 2025)A benchmark of hundreds of original problems; each has one exact answer checked automatically.A script compares the final answer. It certifies the answer, not the reasoning. The paper’s “under 2%” is its original 2024 figure; current scores are not quoted here.
Marinov & Veselinov, arXiv:2602.09253 (9 Feb 2026)An elementary meromorphic function whose derivative has infinitely many roots, at which f takes infinitely many distinct values, is not solvable in elementary functions.A proof in one-dimensional topological Galois theory. The definitions of “solvable” and “elementary” are what the theorem is about.

Cobeli and Zaharescu describe three ingredients for passing 8/9: a spectral remainder of a Gram matrix that the earlier proof dropped, an inequality for seven ordered points summed over blocks of 120, and a choice of parameter that sets the constant. The proofs in their Sections 4–6 were not rechecked here.

The same word, verified, covers a human-read proof, an automated answer check and a kernel check. They fail in different ways.

A kernel can accept a theorem whose conclusion is empty, and a script can accept a number reached by an unsound route. A human reader can miss a step. The series’ standing rule is the one that fits all three: read what the claim says, not what its name promises.

The sandbar, first light
No. 9 The Small, Colorful Demons
Pierre et Mademoiselle, by Pablo Nogueira Grossi  ·  See the note on fact & fiction, below

The buffalo did not so much land them as simply stop, at some point in the grey hour before dawn, in water shallow enough that stopping was possible, and Fermat understood only afterward that this had been its own kind of mercy — that the beast had chosen the moment of arrival as carefully as it had chosen everything else about the crossing, waiting until the storm's worst violence had spent itself before it allowed the two of them to slide, boneless, into the wet sand.

He lay there for a long time without moving, salt-crusted and blind with exhaustion, aware only that Marie was somewhere near him, breathing, which was more than he had let himself hope for during the worst of the swim. When he finally raised his head, the world had gone the flat, colorless grey of very early morning, and the buffalo — his buffalo, he found himself already thinking, with an ownership he had no right to and did not intend to examine — stood a few feet off, utterly unbothered, as though it had done nothing more remarkable than cross a particularly wide puddle.

"We're alive," Marie said. It was not a question, but it did not sound entirely certain either.

"We are extremely alive," Fermat said, "and extremely covered in sand, which I am given to understand is not the same as being safe, but is a considerable improvement on the alternative."

It was only now, with real light finally reaching the flats, that either of them properly saw the thing that had carried them. In the storm it had been a shape, a grip, a fact to be grateful for without examining too closely. In the grey morning it was simply enormous — a vast, slate-hided animal the size of nothing either of them had ever stood beside, horns swept back like something out of an old bestiary, entirely too large to have swum an open, storm-churned sea with two drowning Europeans clinging to its back, and entirely unbothered by having done exactly that.

Marie stared at it for a long moment, then pushed herself upright in the sand, brushing at her ruined skirts with the particular dignity of a woman determined to recover at least one scrap of composure.

"I am extremely grateful to it," she said. "I want that understood. But I would never ride such a creature again. Not for any reason."

"Noted," said Fermat, who had learned, over six years, exactly how much weight to put on Marie's declarations of what she would never do, which was to say: precisely as much weight as the next hour of circumstance allowed her to keep.

⁂

The sun came up fast, the way it seemed to do everywhere in this part of the world, with none of Europe's slow, apologetic dawns, and with the light came the tide's retreat, drawing back off the flats in front of them with a long, hissing sigh — and with the retreating water came the sand itself waking up.

Fermat saw the first claw before he understood what he was seeing: a bright, improbable blue, waving up out of a hole in the mud as though the earth itself had grown a hand. Then another, and another, dozens, then what looked like hundreds, the whole flat suddenly alive and bubbling and clicking with creatures the size of a man's open hand, shells the impossible colors of a court painter's most extravagant palette, advancing across the sand in a slow, scuttling, entirely serious tide of their own.

Marie made a sound he had never heard her make in six years of knowing her — not a court sound, not a composed sound, nothing that Versailles had ever trained into her — and scrambled backward so fast she nearly fell, catching herself instead on the buffalo's foreleg and hauling herself, with a speed her ruined skirts should not have permitted, up onto its broad wet back.

"Those are not natural," she said, from a height she clearly intended to keep.

"They are extremely natural," Fermat said, watching one particularly large specimen wave a claw the color of a robin's egg in what he chose to interpret as a greeting. "Nature has simply declined, in this part of the world, to consult European taste before deciding what a crab should look like."

"I don't care what they are, Pierre, I care that there are a thousand of them and they are coming this way."

He considered the advancing tide of claws with the same flat, assessing attention he had once given a difficult legal brief, weighed it against his own aching, sand-caked, sixty-four-year-old legs, and arrived, not for the first time in recent memory, at a conclusion his life's work had never quite prepared him to phrase so plainly.

"I find," he said, already turning to look for a handhold of his own on the buffalo's broad grey shoulder, "that I would very much rather be eaten by nothing at all than stay here to discover which of us they intend to try first."

The buffalo, for its part, showed no interest whatsoever in the small blue army assembling around its feet. It simply began to walk, unhurried, its great hooves finding the firm ground beneath the mud without any apparent effort, crushing an idle path directly through the scuttling ranks as though they were no more than a particularly loud patch of gravel — carrying an old mathematician and a woman who had once outlasted the sharpest tongues at Versailles, both of them equally undone, for the moment, by an army of crabs.

Fact & Fiction · the ledger for No. 9

Documented, as the series ledger (FACT_AND_FICTION.md) records it; not independently re-checked here. The route La Rochelle, Tortuga, the Guiana coast, Maranhão was a functioning French Atlantic corridor in these years. The buffalo legend of Marajó is a shipwreck around 1890 near Cabo Maguari; its survivors swam ashore. The ledger has no entry for the sandbar, the mangrove flats or the crabs, so this part asserts nothing documented about them.

Invented: the landing on the sandbar, the crabs and the remount, and the buffalo carrying the two travellers onward. The buffalo swimming people ashore is a relocation of the Marajó legend 224 years earlier, as the ledger says for No. 8, not a fabrication of the animal.
