## Main post

**New issue out — *Imaginary Origin*, The Generative Science Journal, Vol. Ω No. 10.**

The lead story is a reflection formula for ζ′/ζ that is not in Mathlib's
library, with sixty similitude cases worked in exact arithmetic and a negative
result that turns out to be the load-bearing one.

But the page I would point students to is the notices page, where I did
something with a job posting instead of only reading it.

Axiom Math is hiring a Member of Technical Staff. I took the posting as a
**specification** and tried to resolve every line of it at an address — not a
claim that I have the skill, but a file, a script, a report a stranger can run.
Then I printed both halves of the result.

**What resolved:** Lean 4 work with an axiom gate rather than a clean compile as
the verdict — 2,234 declarations written, 1,984 free of `sorry`, 173 audited.
Three different denominators, printed together rather than reconciled into one
flattering figure.

**What did not:** large-scale training, reinforcement learning, a learned
proposer searching a space. No training run, no GPU budget, no model of any
size.

And then the part I think is worth more than either list. That second box is not
a list of things I am uninterested in — it is the aim. A proposer that searches
a space and a kernel that checks the results are not equally priced: the check
costs seconds, the search costs a budget. Much of what such a search spends its
compute on is menial — enumerate, discard, re-enumerate — work nobody would pay
a person to do, and which a one-person desk therefore pays for out of pocket or
does not do at all. So the discovery here is done by hand and checked by a
kernel, which is the affordable half of the method rather than the preferred
one.

That is a fact about the meter, not about the mathematics. I printed it because
the alternative is to leave a blank and let a reader infer the wrong reason.

If you are applying for anything this season: half a specification met **at an
address** is more useful to know than a whole one asserted, and the category
that matters most is the third one — the lines you assumed you met until you
went looking for the file. Worth twenty minutes on your own CV.

Read it: https://totogt.github.io/AXLE/Journal/vol10.html

## Short version, if the field is tight

**Vol. Ω No. 10 of *Imaginary Origin* is out** — a reflection formula for ζ′/ζ
that is not in the library, and sixty similitude cases in exact arithmetic.

The notices page carries an exercise worth more to students than the
mathematics. I took a live job posting, read it as a specification, and tried to
resolve every line at an address — a file, a script, something a stranger can
run. Then printed what resolved and what did not.

What did not is the aim, not a disinterest. A kernel check costs seconds; a
learned proposer searching a space costs a budget, and most of what it spends is
on menial enumeration nobody would pay a person for. Hand discovery plus a
kernel is the affordable half of the method.

Try the exercise on your own CV before your next application.

https://totogt.github.io/AXLE/Journal/vol10.html
