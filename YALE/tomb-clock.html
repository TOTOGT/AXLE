What it is doing

The second and the minute are untouched — a minute here is a real minute. Only the hour is redefined as 55 minutes instead of 60. The minute hand sweeps at ordinary speed but wraps at 55, and the hour hand completes its twelve-hour revolution in 12 × 55 = 660 minutes, which is 11 real hours.

That ratio is the whole object: 60/55 = 12/11 ≈ 1.0909. Two different "gains" fall out of it, and they are easy to conflate. The readout rolls its hour over five minutes early, so read naively as h:mm it advances 1h05m per real hour. The hour hand is the honest measure: it sweeps 1/11 of a revolution per real hour against the ordinary 1/12, so it gains 1/132 of a revolution — 60/11 ≈ 5 min 27 s — every hour, and 1440/11 ≈ 2 h 11 m a day.

It cannot be a day system. 1440 = 2⁵ · 3² · 5 has no factor of 11, and 55 = 5 · 11, so no whole number of 55-minute hours tiles a day — 26.18… of them fit. It works only as a rate, running against a reference clock, which is what the five-minutes-ahead story actually describes.

The hour hands align again when that gap closes a whole revolution: 1 ÷ (1/11 − 1/12) = 132 hours — five and a half days. Not six: that figure comes from dividing 720 by the readout's five minutes, which measures the wrong thing.

Offset mode is the rival reading: a clock set five minutes ahead and left alone. It never drifts, and it is not a 55-minute hour at all. The toggle exists because the two get conflated — one is a rate, the other a constant.
