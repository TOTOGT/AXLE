The Reintegration Engine

Arts and sciences, un-siloed — one for all, all for one.
A New Jersey–New York program that is one thing scored two ways: a research study and a public service. G6 LLC · Pablo Nogueira Grossi · Newark, NJ · Draft 2026

We teach four languages as one ladder — English, Portuguese, Mathematics, and Lean — and ask what that ladder does to a mind. The same classroom that gives an immigrant family in Newark free, bilingual instruction in math and formal logic is also a controlled study in how learning across a gradient of formal languages ages the brain. Service and research are not two programs here. They are the same program, read on two axes.

The four languages, one ladder of formalization

Englishnatural language
Portuguêsa second natural language — the bilingual base
Mathematicsthe symbolic language — meaning by structure
Leanthe formal language — meaning a machine can check

Nobody runs a comparative language study across this whole ladder — from ordinary speech, through symbolic notation, to machine-verified proof. That gradient is the novelty.

Intellectual Merit — the research

Question. Does learning up the formality gradient (bilingual → mathematical → formal) compound cognitive reserve beyond what bilingualism alone provides?

Endpoint. A measured cognitive-age gap on standardized batteries, compared across dose levels and against matched controls.

Hypothesis. That the formal-language dose adds to the bilingual effect — a brain measurably younger than chronological age.

Broader Impacts — the service

Who. Immigrant families and people with disabilities — the learners who most often have no reference and no friend in the room. Newark and NYC first.

What. Free, bilingual instruction in mathematics and formal reasoning. All materials open (CC BY-NC-ND). A gift, not a product.

Reach. The curriculum re-instantiates in ~40 languages via LaTeX/babel — the same lesson, any immigrant community, a connected network.

On honesty — the size of the effect The published bilingualism literature points to a delay in cognitive aging of roughly four to five years, and it is contested. A larger effect — a brain a decade or more younger — is our hypothesis to test, justified by the added formal-language dose, not a result to assert. The study is built to let the number land where the data puts it. That honesty is what makes it fundable as science.

Why New Jersey–New York, why now

NSF's newest Regional Innovation Engines span 20 states; none is in New Jersey, and the only New York award sits upstate in Rochester. The NYC–NJ metro corridor — the world's most linguistically diverse metropolitan area (Endangered Language Alliance, languagemap.nyc, 1,300+ mapped language sites including Newark, Clifton, Wayne and Passaic) — is open ground. This is our turf. We want it led from here, and we want it multidisciplinary: arts and sciences reintegrated, not fractured into departments that never speak.

What we bring, what we need

We bring the idea, the curriculum, the four-language method, the open materials, and the community — immigrant parishes and disability-serving networks across Newark and NYC. We need a research anchor: a university or major nonprofit partner eligible to hold the award and run the cognitive-science measurement, with us as the human-capital engine and the community bridge.

A natural anchor — and a living lab The founder studied at Baruch College (CUNY) — among the most linguistically diverse campuses in the country, where by his count some 200 languages are spoken on one Manhattan block of a campus. That is not a backdrop; it is the study population and the translation-scale proof, already assembled. A CUNY/Baruch partnership puts the research anchor, the multilingual cohort, and the founder's own roots in the same place.

The formation ethos

This is what Pope Leo XIV named in Magnifica Humanitas: we teach so that people master the tools of their day — mathematics, formal reasoning, the machines that check it — so that they are the ones using the technology, and are not, later, used by it. Fluency up the formal ladder is not academic ornament; it is the difference between operating the tools of the age and being operated by them. That is the human formation the engine exists to deliver.

And it runs both ways. In the spirit of Freire's Pedagogy of the Oppressed, this is values education, not charity handed down: we teach the tools, and the children of the poor and disenfranchised teach us what the tools are for — what a curriculum built by the comfortable stops being able to see. Methodologically that makes the study community-based participatory research: learners are co-producers of the knowledge, not subjects of it. The reciprocity is the pedagogy and, not by accident, the stronger research design.
