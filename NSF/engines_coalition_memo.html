The NJ–NYC corridor is open ground. How to move on it.

G6 LLC · Pablo Nogueira Grossi · Newark, NJ · Draft 2026 · Facts per NSF Engines news release, July 14, 2026
The gap, in NSF's own numbers NSF's newest cohort funded 12 Engines across 20 states. New Jersey has none. The only New York award is STELLAR, upstate in the Rochester/Finger Lakes region (University of Rochester). Connecticut (quantum, UConn) and New England (seafood) ring the region. The NYC–NJ metropolitan corridor — one of the densest and most multilingual regions in the country — has no Engine. NSF calls the program "a connected national network... filling capability gaps." Human-capital formation in the nation's largest immigrant gateway is a capability gap. The corridor is the world's most linguistically diverse metropolitan area — the Endangered Language Alliance maps 1,300+ language sites across it, NJ included (languagemap.nyc).

How the vehicle actually works

Engines are run out of NSF's TIP directorate (Technology, Innovation & Partnerships) — the use-inspired side that funds research, workforce, and regional economy together. Each award is $15M over two years, scaling to as much as $160M over a decade on milestones. Every Engine is led by a coalition — universities, nonprofits, and private industry — not a single organization. Matching matters: the inaugural nine Engines turned $135M of NSF money into more than $2B in matching commitments. This is a coalition-and-match vehicle, not a grant one applicant wins.

The hard constraint A single LLC cannot lead an Engine. G6's role is coalition member and human-capital engine, not applicant of record. The realistic sequence is: recruit the institutional lead first, contribute the program and the community, and — separately — pursue STTR (company + research institution) as the one NSF door where G6 can itself be the applicant. The realistic on-ramp

  1. Build the coalition now. One institutional lead (start with Baruch/CUNY or Rutgers–Newark) + a workforce partner (NJIT) + community organizations (parishes, disability networks) + at least one industry/philanthropy match commitment.
  2. Enter through a planning/Development-scale award, not the full Engine. NSF has historically offered smaller planning awards (~$1M-scale) as the on-ramp to a full Engine — confirm the current solicitation and next deadline with TIP before counting on it. This is where a young coalition proves the concept.
  3. Run the STTR track in parallel. G6 LLC as applicant, an academic partner as the research institution, to fund the open curriculum + the auto-translating LaTeX platform as a technology — independent of the Engine timeline.
  4. Line up the match early. City programs, the Archdiocese, immigrant and arts foundations, and corporate learning/AI philanthropy are the matching sources; secure letters of commitment before the full-Engine stage.
  5. Talk to a TIP program officer first. Before writing anything, a short call to confirm fit, the live solicitation, and eligibility saves months.
The pitch in one sentence A NYC–NJ Engine that builds the nation's most multilingual workforce by teaching immigrant and disability communities to master mathematics and formal reasoning — research and service in one, in the metro corridor NSF has not yet reached.
