The Cajueiro Principle

O Princípio do Cajueiro

Uceda School of Elizabeth · Elizabeth, NJ
Compress · be here now · the first root
Reading · Leitura

One root. One organism.

The cashew tree of Pirangi, Rio Grande do Norte, is the largest cashew tree in the world. One seed. One root. 8,500 square meters. What looks like a forest is one single organism — a semente tende à floresta. The seed tends to forest.

The Cajueiro Principle takes this as its central image: what if belonging worked the same way? A branch reaches, touches the ground, re-roots, becomes a new trunk. It doesn't stop being part of the first tree. It becomes more of it.

This is the C operator — Comprimir, to compress. Arrive. Be here now. Find the irreducible kernel. You don't have to forget where you came from in order to take root here. That is not how the cajueiro works.

"The cajueiro establishes itself in difficult soil — branches, touches the ground, re-roots, becomes new trunks. One organism. 8,500 m²."

Debate · Discussão

Bring these questions Thursday

The cajueiro re-roots without leaving the original tree. When you moved to a new place, what was the first thing you re-rooted?

Quando você se mudou para um novo lugar, o que foi a primeira coisa que você re-enraizou?

The C operator means: compress to the seed. What is the irreducible thing about you — the part that doesn't change no matter where you plant?

O que é a coisa irredutível em você — a parte que não muda independente de onde você planta?

Is isolation a broken root, or a root that hasn't found its ground yet? What would help it touch down?

O isolamento é uma raiz quebrada, ou uma raiz que ainda não encontrou o chão?
