Reading · Leitura

Four ways into the same knowledge

The Principia Orthogona series is not a textbook. It has four faces — four surfaces of the same mathematical object. You can enter through any of them first. They are not a hierarchy, not a ladder.

This workshop introduces the faces and asks: which one are you? Not everyone learns the same way. The book knows this and offers all four paths in every level.

C · Compress · Read
Ler
The face where the book speaks and you listen. Arrive, be present, find the seed.
K · Curvature · Simulate
Simular
Stand in, play-act, let the body take the part of the thing. Feel the boundary.
F · Fold · Prove
Provar
To prove, to taste, to try on clothes. A proof the body checks.
U · Unfold · Teach
Ensinar
A finger pointing. The unfolding: expansion, the canopy. You carry it forward.

Listening · Escuta

On learning, language, and the body

Before the session, watch this 16-minute TED talk by Dan Finkel on what extraordinary math teaching looks like — and why the standard way often fails. The four faces of the Principia Orthogona are a direct answer to the problems he names.

Also recommended: Read Chapter 1 online and notice which face you naturally reach for — do you read first, simulate, try to prove, or want to teach it immediately?

Debate · Discussão

Bring these questions Thursday

Which of the four faces — Ler, Simular, Provar, Ensinar — is hardest for you in a new language? Does the face change when you switch languages?

Qual das quatro faces é mais difícil para você em um novo idioma?

The book calls you câmara — companion in the circle, not student. What changes when you are a companion instead of a student?

O que muda quando você é câmara ao invés de estudante?

Axiom 9 is Honest Incompleteness — every session leaves one problem in your hands. What problem has immigration left in your hands that school never prepared you for?

Que problema a imigração deixou em suas mãos que a escola nunca o preparou?
