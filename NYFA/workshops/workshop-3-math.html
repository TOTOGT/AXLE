From seed to canopy: the g-series

The g-series is a learning ladder — five stages that describe how any skill, any knowledge, grows from nothing to mastery. It is formally proved in mathematics (Lean 4, Mathlib4), but it also describes what it feels like to learn a new language, navigate a new city, or become part of a new community.

No prior math required. If you can describe the difference between a tourist and someone who belongs — you already understand the g-series.

g⁰
Quiescent — the seed at rest
You've arrived but not yet started. First day in a new country. Full attention, no prior compression.
g²
Nascent oscillation — first branches
Two passes through. Something real has changed but the structure is fragile. You ordered coffee in the new language.
g⁶
Stable micro-cycle — root touches ground
Six passes. A small, stable competence. You can navigate one domain reliably. The g6 moment.
g³³
Stability threshold — the practitioner
Thirty-three consolidated crossings. You think in the domain, not about it. The new city is home.
g⁶⁴
Circuit saturation — the full canopy
Sixty-four passes. The Cajueiro de Pirangi. One seed, 8,500 m², 70 years. The seed tends to forest.
"The g₃₃ moment: cortical activation has reduced, dorsal striatum dominates — the practitioner thinks in the domain, not about it."
