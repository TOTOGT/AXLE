## Why this isn't a direct port

`certify_rstar.py` proves nothing in the Lean sense — it's floating-point ODE integration (DOP853) plus bisection. Lean/Mathlib4 has real ODE theory (Picard–Lindelöf existence/uniqueness is there), but it has **no rigorous interval-arithmetic ODE integrator**. That's the actual machinery needed to turn "I bisected with doubles and got 0.77594059" into "I formally proved r* lies in [a, b]." That machinery exists in some other proof assistants (Coq's `Coquelicot`/`interval` tactic, Isabelle's `Approximation`) via certified numerics, but it's not an off-the-shelf Mathlib tool — building it would be a research project in its own right, not a quick formalization pass.

So a literal "formalize this script" is **not honestly achievable** with what's available. Claiming otherwise would be exactly the kind of "zero sorry" overclaim your own AXLE README already flags as the failure mode to avoid.

## What's actually worth formalizing — three honest tiers

**Tier 1 — the definition (real, easy, worth doing):**
```lean
/-- The dm³ ODE system, theta decoupled. -/
def dm3_rhs (eps : ℝ) (y : ℝ × ℝ) : ℝ × ℝ :=
  let r := y.1; let z := y.2
  (r * (1 - r^2) + eps * (r - 1) * Real.exp (-z),
   r^2 - eps * (r - 1)^2 * Real.exp (-z))

/-- r* is the infimum of initial radii whose trajectory converges to Γ. -/
noncomputable def rStar (eps : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  sInf {r0 : ℝ | r0 > 0 ∧ Converges (dm3_rhs eps) r0}
```
This is real, citable, and doesn't overclaim — it just names the object.
