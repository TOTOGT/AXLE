Here is a formal, professional template for your Discovery Request Letter.
In New Jersey municipal courts, this is called a Rule 7:7-7 Discovery Demand. You must mail or hand-deliver this to the municipal prosecutor immediately so they have time to pull the records before your court date.
------------------------------
[Your Name]
[Your Street Address]
[Your City, State, Zip Code]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email Address]
[Date]
Office of the Municipal Prosecutor
Municipal Court of [Insert Name of City/Township, e.g., Newark]
[Court Street Address]
[Court City, State, Zip Code]
RE: State of New Jersey v. [Your Name]
Ticket/Summons Number(s): [Insert All Ticket Numbers here, separated by commas]
Arraignment/Court Date: [Insert Your Next Court Date]
FORMAL DEMAND FOR DISCOVERY PURSUANT TO NEW JERSEY COURT RULE 7:7-7
To the Municipal Prosecutor:
I am the Defendant in the above-referenced matter, representing myself pro se. Pursuant to New Jersey Court Rule 7:7-7, I hereby formally demand the disclosure and production of all relevant discovery materials, exculpatory evidence, and records within the possession, custody, or control of the State, the municipal police department, and the [Insert Name of Parking Authority, e.g., Newark Parking Authority].
Specifically, this matter involves parking tickets issued at or near parking meter/payment station number [Insert Machine/Meter Number if known, or describe exact location, e.g., Meter #42 on Broad Street] on the following dates: [List all dates you got tickets, e.g., October 12, 2024, November 3, 2024].
To prepare my defense regarding the operational failure and lack of maintenance of the physical payment infrastructure, I demand production of the following specific items:

   1. Maintenance & Service Logs: All routine maintenance records, repair logs, and service tickets for the subject parking machine/meter covering the period from 30 days prior to the first ticket date through the date of the last ticket.
   2. Error & System Logs: Digital or physical error logs, network disconnection reports, hardware failure logs, and cash-vault full/jammed alerts generated by or for the subject machine on the specific dates of the alleged violations.
   3. Audit & Transaction Records: Hourly transaction reports or coin/bill count audits for the subject machine on the dates of the violations, to prove whether physical cash transactions were being successfully processed by the machine.
   4. Rate and Ordinance Data: Copy of the current approved municipal ordinance or parking authority resolution governing the cash-discount and electronic-payment rate structures for this specific location.
   5. Officer Notes: Copies of the issuing parking enforcement officer’s logs, notes, or digital ticket histories for the dates of issuance.

Please send these materials to my attention at the address listed at the top of this letter. If the State or the Parking Authority cannot produce these records, or if the records are non-existent, please provide a written certification stating the same.
Thank you for your prompt attention to this matter.
Sincerely,

signature: _________________________

[Print Your Name]
Defendant Pro Se
------------------------------
## Crucial Next Steps:

* Make Three Copies: Keep one for your own files, mail one to the Prosecutor, and keep one to hand to the Judge if the prosecutor claims they "never received it."
* Send it via Certified Mail: Send it with Return Receipt Requested at the post office. This gives you a green signature card proving the prosecutor's office received it.
* If they ignore you: When you stand before the Judge on your court date, tell the judge: "Your Honor, I sent a formal Rule 7:7-7 discovery demand for the machine's maintenance logs via Certified Mail on [Date], and the state has failed to provide them. I request a postponement or a dismissal." Judges will usually force the prosecutor to comply.
